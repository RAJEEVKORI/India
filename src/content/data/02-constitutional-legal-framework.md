## 1. Constitutional & Linguistic Facts
* The Constitution of India was adopted on November 26, 1949, and came into force on January 26, 1950, establishing a Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic per the [Legislative Department, Ministry of Law and Justice](https://legislative.gov.in/constitution-of-india/).
* India enacted Universal Adult Suffrage from the first day of the Republic in 1950 under Article 326, granting equal voting rights to every citizen aged 18 and above as overseen by the [Election Commission of India](https://www.eci.gov.in/).
* The Constitution does not declare a single "National Language"; Article 343 designates Hindi in Devanagari script and English as the official languages of the Union Government per the [Department of Official Language](https://rajbhasha.gov.in/).
* The Eighth Schedule of the Constitution officially recognizes 22 scheduled languages: Assamese, Bengali, Bodo, Dogri, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Kashmiri, Konkani, Maithili, Malayalam, Manipuri, Marathi, Nepali, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, Santali, Sindhi, Tamil, Telugu, and Urdu per the [Ministry of Home Affairs](https://www.mha.gov.in/).
* The Government of India officially designates 11 Classical Languages (Tamil, Sanskrit, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Odia, Marathi, Pali, Prakrit, Assamese, and Bengali) for their ancient literary heritage via the [Ministry of Culture](https://www.indiaculture.gov.in/).
* The Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2019 (CAA) provides an accelerated naturalization pathway exclusively for persecuted religious minorities from Afghanistan, Bangladesh, and Pakistan who entered India on or before December 31, 2014, and contains zero provisions affecting existing Indian citizens per the [Official Gazette of India](https://egazette.gov.in/).
